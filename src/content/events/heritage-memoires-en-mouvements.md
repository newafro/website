## About the event

Everything moves, and memory is transmission itself.

Everything is movement. Humanity itself is movement, the very core of our existence. To borrow the words of Achille Mbembe, the history of humanity is a history of circulation.

But if everything moves, what remains of us? What endures of the body, of being, of memory? Who are we when everything around us shifts? What shapes our identities, both personal and collective? What is left when things relocate, transform, or fade?

Our multiple inheritances shape how we see the world, how we see others, and how we understand ourselves. They inhabit us, traverse us, and often define us in silence. At a time when identity-driven narratives sometimes overlook the interconnectedness of our memories, when the past is reshaped, silenced, or erased, intangible heritage and transmission become crucial. In an era marked by conversations on restitution, repair, development, reconstruction, and transformation, it becomes urgent to question what cannot be touched: intimate memory, silent inheritance, the archives of the heart. Movement can illuminate, but it can also wound. So we must ask: Where are we going? If the present overwrites the past, what will our futures look like? What will remain of us?

This desire to open conversations, and to root them in the African continent and its youth, led to the creation of the itinerant project "Restitution... et après?" (Restitution... and then?).

The project reflects on the future of restitutions and their real impact on contemporary African societies navigating modernity, transformation, tradition, and inherited memory.

Its first iteration took place in May 2024 at the Institut français in Mainz, where four multidisciplinary artists explored future imaginaries through an Afro-futurist lens (Chelsea Odofu, Chidi Nwaubani, Ibaaku, Nuits Balnéaires).

In Cotonou, the project's first stop outside Europe, I invite Nuits Balnéaires and Tobi Onabolu, two artists of striking sensitivity and aesthetic clarity, to engage with the themes of identity, heritage, memory, and movement.

Together, they open a dialogue between material and immaterial archives. Through "Love Is What Remains When We Have Forgotten Everything: Archives of the Heart" and "The Constant Is Flux", they expand the limits of collective memory and awaken echoes of the past within us.

Who are we, when everything is in motion?
